# Abstract Templates (5 Formats)

---

## 1. Structured Abstract (Medical / Clinical — AMA/IMRAD style)
*~250 words. Common in: JAMA, NEJM, BMJ, Lancet*

**Background:** [1–2 sentences on the problem and why it matters]

**Objective:** [1 sentence: "To [verb] [what] in [population/context]"]

**Methods:** [Study design, setting, participants, intervention/exposure, outcome measures, analysis]

**Results:** [Key quantitative findings with statistics. Lead with your most important result.]

**Conclusions:** [What the findings mean. Clinical/practical implication. Limitations if space allows.]

**Keywords:** [5–8 keywords, including MeSH terms if relevant]

---

## 2. Unstructured Abstract (Humanities / Social Sciences)
*~150–200 words. Common in: JSTOR journals, humanities venues*

[Single paragraph. Flow: context → gap/problem → argument/contribution → methods (briefly) → findings → significance. No subheadings.]

**Keywords:** [4–6 terms]

---

## 3. Conference Abstract (STEM)
*~200–300 words. Often has a hard word limit.*

**[Title — specific and informative, not clever]**

[Background: 2–3 sentences framing the problem]

[Objective: 1 sentence]

[Methods: 2–4 sentences — enough to evaluate rigor]

[Results: Most important finding(s) with numbers if available]

[Conclusion: 1–2 sentences on implication]

*Note: Many conferences want results even for "work in progress" — include preliminary data if available.*

---

## 4. Dissertation / Thesis Abstract
*~300–500 words. Stands completely alone.*

**Chapter 1 framing:** [What problem does this dissertation address and why does it matter?]

**Gap:** [What is missing from existing knowledge?]

**Research questions:** [State them explicitly — 1–3 RQs or a central argument]

**Methodology:** [Approach in 2–3 sentences — qualitative, quantitative, mixed, theoretical?]

**Key findings:** [One paragraph summarizing major results across chapters]

**Contributions:** [What does this add? Theoretical / empirical / methodological contribution]

**Implications:** [For practice, policy, future research]

*Tip: Write the abstract last. Every sentence should earn its place.*

---

## 5. Grant Abstract / Project Summary
*~30 lines / ~500 words (NIH format). Three required sections.*

**Overview (~1 paragraph):**
[State the problem, the long-term goal, and the objective of this specific project]

**Relevance:**
[Why does this matter to public health / science / society? Write for a non-specialist.]

**Specific Aims Summary:**
[1–2 sentences per aim. What will you do and what do you expect to find?]

*Note: The NIH project summary is public record. Avoid sensitive/confidential details. Write accessibly.*
