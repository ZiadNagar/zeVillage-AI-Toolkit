# NIH-Style Specific Aims Page Template

*Target: 1 page. This is the most important page of your grant. Write it last.*

---

## Hook (1–2 sentences)

State the broad health/scientific problem with urgency and significance. Avoid jargon. Example:

> "Alzheimer's disease affects 6.7 million Americans and lacks a disease-modifying treatment, creating an urgent need to identify targetable mechanisms of neurodegeneration."

---

## Long-Term Goal (1 sentence)

> "Our long-term goal is to [broad ambition that extends beyond this grant period]."

---

## Overall Objective (1–2 sentences)

> "The objective of this application is to [specific, achievable goal of THIS grant]."

---

## Central Hypothesis (1 sentence)

> "Our central hypothesis is that [testable, falsifiable statement], based on [preliminary data / strong rationale]."

---

## Rationale (1–2 sentences)

> "The rationale for the proposed research is that [why success here matters — what changes in the field if the hypothesis is confirmed or refuted]."

---

## Innovation & Significance (closing paragraph)

> "This research is innovative because [what's genuinely new — method, model, population, question]. The proposed work is significant because [impact on the field, on policy, on health outcomes]. At the completion of these aims, we expect to [deliverable outcome], thereby [bigger-picture impact]."

---

## Tips

- **Verbs to use for aims:** Determine, Establish, Test, Evaluate, Identify, Characterize, Examine, Quantify, Develop
- **Verbs to avoid:** Study, Investigate, Explore (too vague)
- Aims should be related but **not dependent** on each other (if Aim 1 fails, Aims 2 and 3 should still be doable)
- Use **bold** sparingly to highlight the 3–4 most critical phrases
- Page should be scannable in 90 seconds by a tired reviewer
