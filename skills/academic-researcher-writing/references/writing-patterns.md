# Academic Writing Patterns by Section

Sentence-level patterns for each IMRaD section. Use these as starting points, not formulas.

---

## Introduction Patterns

### Establishing the field (Move 1)
- "[Field X] has attracted considerable attention in recent years due to..."
- "Understanding [phenomenon] is central to [broader goal] because..."
- "The relationship between [A] and [B] has been a subject of debate since [year]..."

### Establishing a niche / gap (Move 2)
- "However, little is known about..."
- "Despite extensive research on [A], [B] remains poorly understood."
- "Previous studies have focused primarily on [X], leaving [Y] unexplored."
- "While [claim], no study has examined [specific gap]."
- "Existing models fail to account for [phenomenon] in [context]."

### Occupying the niche / stating purpose (Move 3)
- "The present study aims to..."
- "To address this gap, we..."
- "This paper proposes / presents / reports..."
- "We hypothesize that..."

## Methods Patterns

### Describing participants / data
- "Participants were recruited from [setting] using [sampling method]."
- "The dataset comprised [N] [units] collected between [dates]."
- "Inclusion criteria were: [list]. Exclusion criteria were: [list]."

### Describing procedure
- "Data were collected using [instrument/protocol]."
- "Participants completed [task] under [conditions]."
- "[Model / algorithm] was applied to [data] to [purpose]."

### Describing analysis
- "Data were analyzed using [software, version]."
- "We employed [method] to examine [RQ]."
- "Statistical significance was set at α = 0.05."
- "Thematic analysis followed the six-phase framework of Braun and Clarke (2006)."

---

## Results Patterns

### Leading with the finding
- "Analysis revealed that..."
- "[Variable A] was significantly associated with [Variable B] (r = .XX, p < .001)."
- "Three themes emerged from the data: [A], [B], and [C]."
- "The model achieved [metric] of [value] on the test set."

### Reporting non-significant / null results
- "No significant difference was found between [groups] ([stat], p = .XX)."
- "Contrary to our hypothesis, [outcome] did not differ significantly..."
- "The effect of [X] on [Y] was not significant, though the direction was consistent with..."

### Directing to figures/tables
- "As shown in Figure X..."
- "Table X presents the [means/frequencies/results] for each condition."
- "The distribution of [variable] is illustrated in Figure X."

---

## Discussion Patterns

### Restating the key finding (briefly)
- "The primary finding of this study is that..."
- "This study set out to examine [RQ]. The results indicate that..."

### Relating to existing literature
- "This finding is consistent with [Author, Year], who reported..."
- "In contrast to [Author, Year], our results suggest..."
- "These results extend the work of [Author, Year] by..."
- "While [Author] found [X] in [context], the current study found [Y] in [context], which may reflect..."

### Explaining unexpected findings
- "The unexpected [finding] may be explained by..."
- "One possible explanation for this result is..."
- "This discrepancy might be attributed to differences in [sample/method/context]."

### Acknowledging limitations
- "Several limitations of this study should be noted."
- "First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference."
- "The sample was drawn from [specific group], which limits generalizability to..."
- "Future research should address these limitations by..."

### Stating implications
- "These findings have important implications for [theory / practice / policy]."
- "Practitioners in [field] may benefit from..."
- "This study contributes to theory by demonstrating that..."

### Closing
- "In conclusion, this study provides evidence that..."
- "Taken together, these findings suggest that..."
- "Future research should examine [next question], particularly in [context]."

## Hedging Language (Use where appropriate)

| Claim strength | Language                                               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Strong         | "demonstrates", "shows", "establishes"                 |
| Moderate       | "suggests", "indicates", "supports the view that"      |
| Cautious       | "may", "might", "appears to", "is consistent with"     |
| Very cautious  | "it is possible that", "preliminary evidence suggests" |

*Match hedging to the strength of your evidence. Over-hedging weakens papers; under-hedging overstates findings.*

## Common Academic Writing Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This paper will discuss X"              | "This paper examines X" (present tense for paper itself) |
| Starting abstract with "This paper..."   | Start with the problem or finding                        |
| "It is interesting to note that..."      | Delete — just note the thing                             |
| "In conclusion, in summary, to conclude" | Pick one; never stack them                               |
| Passive voice overuse (some fields)      | Check field norms; CS/ML often uses active               |
| Undefined acronyms                       | Define on first use, even in abstract                    |
| "Prove" for empirical work               | Use "demonstrate", "show", "provide evidence"            |
| Citation at end of long paragraph        | Break up; cite specific claims                           |
