## Search Strategies

### Snowballing
1. Find one highly-relevant "seed" paper
2. Check its **reference list** (backward snowball — older papers)
3. Check **"cited by"** in Google Scholar (forward snowball — newer papers)
4. Repeat for high-value papers you find

### Systematic Review Protocol
1. Pre-register search strategy (PROSPERO for health sciences)
2. Search ≥2 databases with identical queries
3. Document: query strings, databases, date of search, filters
4. Screen titles → abstracts → full text (two independent reviewers for rigorous reviews)
5. Extract data with a standardized form (synthesis matrix)
6. Assess bias (ROBINS-I, Cochrane RoB, or equivalent)
7. Report with PRISMA flow diagram

### Citation Alert Setup
- **Google Scholar**: Search → Create alert (envelope icon)
- **Semantic Scholar**: Follow paper → get email on new citations
- **ResearchGate**: Follow authors
- **ORCID**: Follow researchers' new publications

## Access Tips

If you hit a paywall:
1. Check if **open-access version** exists on ArXiv, SSRN, author's website, or institutional repository
2. Use **Unpaywall** browser extension (legal open-access)
3. Use **Open Access Button** (oa.works)
4. Request via **ResearchGate** (author direct request)
5. Check **PubMed Central** (PMC) — federally funded US research is often deposited there
6. **Library access**: Most university libraries offer remote access via VPN/proxy
7. **Interlibrary loan (ILL)**: Your library can often obtain papers within 24–48 hours

**Note:** Sci-Hub operates in a legal gray area and is blocked in many jurisdictions. The above legal options usually provide access to the same papers.
